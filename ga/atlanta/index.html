Atlanta, Georgia frost dates and planting calendar

By Which Frost. Last verified 16 Nov 2026.

The median last spring freeze at 32°F is March 24. The median first fall freeze at 32°F is November 8. The median growing season at 32°F is 231 days (the season is at least this long in 50 percent of years).

Station Atlanta Fulton CO AP, GA US (USW00003888), 5.8 miles from the city center. Elevation 256.0 m (840 ft, converted from the NOAA meters). NOAA used 22 years for this element. Completeness flag R: Representative. NOAA had at least 10 years, and could fill missing months from nearby stations.

The USDA hardiness zone for ZIP 30314, the zoned ZIP whose centroid is nearest the city center (0.6 miles away), is 8a (10 to 15°F).

These figures are 1991 to 2020 climate normals, not a forecast. A given year can freeze earlier or later. This is not legal advice, not insurance guidance, and not agricultural advice. Census 2024 population estimate: 520,070.

Freeze probability dates

Thresholds are the minimum temperature. 36°F is a light frost in many extension guides, 32°F is a freeze, and 28°F is a harder freeze. Blank results use NOAA's own codes, described under the tables. Spring 90 percent is an earlier date (a freeze is still likely). Spring 10 percent is a later date (only a 10 percent chance the last freeze is still to come).

Planting calendar

From Vegetable Garden Calendar, University of Georgia Extension Circular 943, full review 10 March 2026. UGA Extension Circular 943 says to treat middle Georgia as the belt from Columbus through Macon to Augusta. This city is north of the Columbus, Macon, and Augusta belt. UGA says spring dates are 1 to 3 weeks later in north Georgia, and fall dates about 2 weeks earlier. The months below stay the middle Georgia baseline. This page does not shift them. UGA says the recommendations follow long term average last and first killing frost dates, and that a given year may not match the average.

The full year is listed below. With JavaScript on, the current month is marked.

January

Plan the garden, order seed, and take a soil sample. Get plant beds ready so tomatoes, peppers, and eggplant can be seeded in early February.

February

Start seed boxes. UGA says peppers and eggplant take 8 weeks from seed to transplant size, and tomatoes take 6 weeks. Early plantings: carrots, collards, lettuce, mustard, English peas, Irish potatoes, radishes, spinach, and turnips.

March

Second plantings of turnips, mustard, radishes, and spring onions. Have rows ready for warm season crops in the last week of March or the first week or two of April, as the weather allows.

April

Plant frost tender crops: snap, pole, and lima beans, cantaloupe, sweet corn, cucumbers, eggplant, okra, field peas, peppers, squash, tomatoes, and watermelon.

May

A third planting of snap beans, corn, squash, and lima beans. Keep mulch and water in place for the crops already growing.

June

Plant sweet potatoes and a second planting of Southern peas. Harvest beans, peas, squash, cucumbers, and okra often.

July

UGA says to plant tomatoes, okra, corn, pole beans, lima beans, cucumbers, squash, and snap beans no later than July 20 if they are to mature before frost.

August

Snap beans and Irish potatoes no later than August 15. Cucumbers and squash no later than August 31. Start broccoli, cabbage, cauliflower, collards, kale, and onions for setting out in September.

September

Plant or transplant beets, broccoli, cabbage, carrots, collards, lettuce, mustard, onions, radishes, spinach, and turnips. UGA groups September and October together for this list.

October

Same cool season list as September: beets, broccoli, cabbage, carrots, collards, lettuce, mustard, onions, radishes, spinach, and turnips.

November

UGA's November and December notes are about manure, leaves, soil samples, and seed orders. They do not name a planting list.

December

Order next year's seed. UGA does not name a planting list for December.

Frequently asked questions

What does a frost date mean in Atlanta?

The median last spring freeze at 32°F is March 24. The median first fall freeze at 32°F is November 8. When NOAA publishes a date, the spring figure is the chance the last spring freeze happens on or after that day, and the fall figure is the chance the first fall freeze happens on or before that day. A 50 percent date is the median for 1991 to 2020. It is not a forecast for this year.

Why can Atlanta have more than one weather station?

This page uses Atlanta Fulton CO AP, GA US (USW00003888), the nearest NOAA normals station with a published frost product, 5.8 miles from the city center. Another station in the same area can sit at a different elevation or closer to water, and NOAA can publish a different date there. Which Frost does not average stations together.

What should I do about a late frost in Atlanta?

In the 1991 to 2020 normals, NOAA gives a 10 percent chance that the last 32°F freeze in this record is still to come on or after April 10. Planting on the 50 percent date (March 24) still leaves a later freeze possible. This page is not a forecast. If a freeze is predicted after you plant, wait or protect seedlings. That is not agricultural advice.
