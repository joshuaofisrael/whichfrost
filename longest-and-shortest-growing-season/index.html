How this list is built

The growing season figure is NOAA's own prbgsl-t32fp50 element from the same 1991 to 2020 Climate Normals by station archive every city page on this site cites, read from the same per station cache (see sources and methods). It is not calculated here by subtracting the spring and fall dates; NOAA publishes it as its own figure, and a station can carry it even where the separate spring or fall date is not computed.

Frequently asked questions

What does a 365 day growing season mean?

NOAA's growing season length at 32°F is the number of days, in the middle year of the 1991 to 2020 record, between the last spring freeze and the first fall freeze at that station. A figure of 365 days means the station's own history did not show a 32°F freeze often enough, in at least half of those years, for NOAA to compute a season shorter than the full year. It does not mean a 32°F reading is impossible there, only that NOAA's median figure for this element is the full year.

Why does a city with a 365 day season still say Not computed for the spring frost date?

Growing season length and the last spring 32°F date are two different NOAA elements, computed from different parts of the station record. A station can have enough data for NOAA to publish a growing season figure while not having enough qualifying years to publish a separate last spring 32°F date, which NOAA then flags -9999. This site's cities with no computed 32°F frost date page lists which stations that happens at.

Does this list change often?

No. It is built from the NOAA 1991 to 2020 climate normals, the same one time retrieval every city page on this site uses. NOAA does not update that record until the next normals period, expected in 2030.
