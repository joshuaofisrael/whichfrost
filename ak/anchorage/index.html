Anchorage, Alaska frost dates and planting calendar

By Which Frost. Last verified 20 Nov 2026.

The median last spring freeze at 32°F is May 16. The median first fall freeze at 32°F is September 20. The median growing season at 32°F is 125 days (the season is at least this long in 50 percent of years).

Station Eagle Rvr Nature Ctr, AK US (USC00502642), 4.2 miles from the city center. Elevation 158.5 m (520 ft, converted from the NOAA meters). NOAA used 9 years for this element. Completeness flag E: Estimated. NOAA had at least 2 years, and estimated the normal from nearby stations that have a standard record.

The USDA hardiness zone for ZIP 99577, the zoned ZIP whose centroid is nearest the city center (under 1 mile away), is 5a (-20 to -15°F).

These figures are 1991 to 2020 climate normals, not a forecast and not a guarantee for this year. A given year can freeze earlier or later. This is not legal advice, not insurance guidance, and not agricultural advice. Census 2024 population estimate: 289,600.

Freeze probability dates

Thresholds are the minimum temperature. 36°F is a light frost in many extension guides, 32°F is a freeze, and 28°F is a harder freeze. Blank results use NOAA's own codes, described under the tables. Spring 90 percent is an earlier date (a freeze is still likely). Spring 10 percent is a later date (only a 10 percent chance the last freeze is still to come).

What 36°F, 32°F, and 28°F mean on this station

The 50 percent spring dates above are May 31 at 36°F, May 16 at 32°F, and May 1 at 28°F. The 50 percent first fall date at 28°F is October 1. Those are 1991 to 2020 medians at Eagle River Nature Center, not a warning for a given night, and not a guarantee that this year will match them.

The NWS glossary describes frost as ice crystals on the ground or on plants. It often shows up with the thermometer still in the middle 30s, because the surface cools below the air. A frost advisory, in that glossary, is for widespread frost in the growing season, usually with surface temperatures in the middle 30s. NWS Raleigh publishes a local version of the same idea: 33 to 36°F for a frost advisory, 32°F or lower for a freeze warning, and 28°F or lower for a hard freeze. That office's cutoffs are not a national rule. Other offices set their own.

The glossary entry for a freeze is surface air expected at 32°F or below over a wide area for a climatologically significant time. A hard freeze, on the same page, is 28°F or lower for an extended period, which the glossary says kills most vegetation. The NWS cold weather page uses the same split for action: a freeze warning when the forecast goes below 32°F for a long period (some crops and garden plants die), and it says temperatures below 28°F for an extended period can kill most of them. On this station the median last 28°F date and the median last 32°F date are 15 days apart (May 1 versus May 16).

A separate Hard Freeze Warning is no longer its own product. Service Change Notice 23-44 (updated 26 March 2024) set the change for on or about 1 October 2024: the hard freeze watch and warning were folded into the freeze watch and warning, and the warning text can still say "hard freeze" when the forecast low is 28°F or lower. The NWS hazard simplification note describes the same consolidation. Both were checked 20 Nov 2026.

That 125 day season is the second shortest among 341 cities on this site with a computed NOAA figure. Only Bend, Oregon, is shorter, at 105 days. Both figures are NOAA's own growing season element, listed on the season ranking. This site does not subtract the spring date from the fall date to invent a third number.

UAF Cooperative Extension HGA-00047 (Growing Garlic in Alaska, revised 27 May 2025, checked 20 Nov 2026) calls a killing frost the first autumn night the air reaches about 32°F, and says to plant garlic a week or two after that, or four to six weeks before the ground freezes, generally from the middle of September to the middle of October. This station's median first 32°F date is September 20, inside that wide window. UAF points gardeners to the Alaska Garden Helper for a local date. This page does not turn the garlic note into one planting day.

Check another zone 5a city

Anchorage is USDA zone 5a, the same winter band as Minneapolis (last 32°F April 23, 175 days), Madison (May 1, 161 days), and Billings (May 4, 156 days). The frost dates are not the same number. The Alaska page lists every zone 5a city on this site, and Bend is the short season comparison, in zone 6b rather than 5a.

A USDA zone is the average coldest winter temperature, not a planting day. These dates are medians, not a guarantee for this year.

Planting calendar

The same publication says beans freeze easily and that an early bean planting needs cover when the night falls near freezing, and it lists corn only under plastic. The span is 14 days before the NOAA 50 percent date through that date.

UAF's worked example counts six weeks back from a 1 June last killing frost and lands on 20 April. This table uses that counting rule with this city's NOAA 50 percent date, not 1 June. HGA-00134 says the Anchorage area, the Kenai Peninsula, and Bristol Bay have low soil temperatures because wind off the water moderates the summer, and it names clear plastic mulch for warm soil crops such as corn, tomatoes, and squash. A garden in a frost pocket, it says, should be planted only with frost resistant vegetables, and a few feet of extra elevation can add days to the season. Outside the Interior, UAF says few sites ripen tomatoes, except favored spots in Anchorage and the Matanuska Valley under plastic mulch. Peat pots are not recommended, because they do not break down in cold Alaska soils. These counted dates are medians plus extension offsets, not a forecast and not agricultural advice.

Frequently asked questions

What does a frost date mean in Anchorage?

The median last spring freeze at 32°F is May 16. The median first fall freeze at 32°F is September 20. When NOAA publishes a date, the spring figure is the chance the last spring freeze happens on or after that day, and the fall figure is the chance the first fall freeze happens on or before that day. A 50 percent date is the median for 1991 to 2020. It is not a forecast for this year.

Why can Anchorage have more than one weather station?

This page uses Eagle Rvr Nature Ctr, AK US (USC00502642), the nearest NOAA normals station with a published frost product, 4.2 miles from the city center. Another station in the same area can sit at a different elevation or closer to water, and NOAA can publish a different date there. Which Frost does not average stations together.

What should I do about a late frost in Anchorage?

In the 1991 to 2020 normals, NOAA gives a 10 percent chance that the last 32°F freeze in this record is still to come on or after June 9. Planting on the 50 percent date (May 16) still leaves a later freeze possible. This page is not a forecast. If a freeze is predicted after you plant, wait or protect seedlings. That is not agricultural advice.

Why is the Anchorage date an estimate?

NOAA's completeness flag on the 32°F spring element at Eagle River Nature Center is E, and the file lists 9 years. Flag E means NOAA had at least 2 years and estimated the normal from nearby stations that have a standard record. Which Frost prints that estimate. It does not swap in a different station.

Does zone 5a give Anchorage the same frost date as Minneapolis?

No. Both are USDA zone 5a on this site, a winter minimum of -20 to -15°F. Minneapolis's median last spring freeze at 32°F is April 23, and its median growing season is 175 days. Anchorage's median last spring freeze at 32°F is May 16, and its season is 125 days. The zone is not a planting day.
