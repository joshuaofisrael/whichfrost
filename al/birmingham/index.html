Birmingham, Alabama frost dates and planting calendar

By Which Frost. Last verified 21 Nov 2026.

The median last spring freeze at 32°F is March 21. The median first fall freeze at 32°F is November 11. The median growing season at 32°F is 236 days (the season is at least this long in 50 percent of years).

Station Birmingham AP, AL US (USW00013876), 4.0 miles from the city center. Elevation 187.5 m (615 ft, converted from the NOAA meters). NOAA used 28 years for this element. Completeness flag S: Standard. NOAA had 24 or more years, and could fill missing months from nearby stations.

The USDA hardiness zone for ZIP 35203, the zoned ZIP whose centroid is nearest the city center (1.0 miles away), is 8a (10 to 15°F).

These figures are 1991 to 2020 climate normals, not a forecast and not a guarantee for this year. A given year can freeze earlier or later. This is not legal advice, not insurance guidance, and not agricultural advice. Census 2024 population estimate: 196,357.

Freeze probability dates

Thresholds are the minimum temperature. 36°F is a light frost in many extension guides, 32°F is a freeze, and 28°F is a harder freeze. Blank results use NOAA's own codes, described under the tables. Spring 90 percent is an earlier date (a freeze is still likely). Spring 10 percent is a later date (only a 10 percent chance the last freeze is still to come).

What the three Birmingham dates are for

The 50 percent spring dates at this station are April 3 at 36°F, March 21 at 32°F, and March 7 at 28°F. The 10 percent spring date at 32°F is April 8, 18 days after the median. That count is the calendar gap between two NOAA probability dates. It is not a forecast that a freeze will land on April 8 this year, and it is not NOAA's growing season figure.

On 21 Nov 2026 the NCEI product page still called the 1991 to 2020 normals the latest set, said a new set is produced about every 10 years, and said the 2023 v1.0.1 update left agricultural normals unchanged. The freeze dates in the tables above were not replaced. The station file is still the 16 Nov 2026 retrieval.

Among the five Alabama cities on this site, Mobile has the earliest median last 32°F freeze, February 21, and Huntsville has the latest, March 24. Birmingham's median is 28 days after Mobile and 3 days before Huntsville. Tuscaloosa is 3 days earlier than Birmingham. Each figure is one station's median. This site does not average them into a state date. The Alabama page puts the five rows side by side.

Alabama Cooperative Extension (Shane Harris, posted 21 April 2021, checked 21 Nov 2026) says warm season vegetables such as tomatoes and peppers can start to show leaf damage at 33°F, and that some cool season crops can take 26 to 31°F for a limited time. On this station the 36°F median (April 3) falls later than the 32°F median (March 21). A reader who uses only the 32°F headline can still be inside the part of spring when a frost advisory, which the NWS glossary ties to surface temperatures in the middle 30s, is an ordinary event in the climate record. How to protect plants from frost collects what that Alabama page, Texas A&M, and UC say about covers, soil water, and pots. It does not change the dates on this page. This is not agricultural advice.

Check another Alabama city

Birmingham is USDA zone 8a. The other large cities on this site are Mobile (February 21, zone 9a), Huntsville (March 24, zone 8a), Montgomery (March 4), and Tuscaloosa (March 18). Same state, different medians. Open a row before treating Birmingham's March 21 date as the date for the coast or for Huntsville.

Frequently asked questions

What does a frost date mean in Birmingham?

The median last spring freeze at 32°F is March 21. The median first fall freeze at 32°F is November 11. When NOAA publishes a date, the spring figure is the chance the last spring freeze happens on or after that day, and the fall figure is the chance the first fall freeze happens on or before that day. A 50 percent date is the median for 1991 to 2020. It is not a forecast for this year.

Why can Birmingham have more than one weather station?

This page uses Birmingham AP, AL US (USW00013876), the nearest NOAA normals station with a published frost product, 4.0 miles from the city center. Another station in the same area can sit at a different elevation or closer to water, and NOAA can publish a different date there. Which Frost does not average stations together.

What should I do about a late frost in Birmingham?

In the 1991 to 2020 normals, NOAA gives a 10 percent chance that the last 32°F freeze in this record is still to come on or after April 8. Planting on the 50 percent date (March 21) still leaves a later freeze possible. This page is not a forecast. If a freeze is predicted after you plant, wait or protect seedlings. That is not agricultural advice. Extension steps for covers, soil water, and pots, and the temperatures those pages name, are summarized on this site's frost protection guide. That guide is not a forecast.
