USDA zone 6b: what to plant now

By Which Frost. Last verified 19 Nov 2026.

Zone 6b on the 2023 USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map is an average annual extreme minimum of -5 to 0°F. It is not a frost date and not a sowing date.

Frost dates inside zone 6b

USDA zone 6b is a winter minimum band. The frost dates below are NOAA 1991 to 2020 medians at 32°F for the 27 large cities on this site that fall in that band. They are medians, not guarantees for any single year.

Across 27 cities with a computed spring date, the earliest median last spring 32°F freeze is April 4 in Kansas City, Missouri. The latest is June 6 in Bend, Oregon.

NOAA's median growing season at 32°F in this zone runs from 105 days in Bend, Oregon to 211 days in Kansas City, Missouri.

Frequently asked questions

Does USDA zone 6b tell me what to plant this week?

No. Zone 6b is the 2023 USDA average annual extreme minimum temperature, -5 to 0°F. Nurseries use it for which perennial plants can take a typical winter. Vegetable sowing dates on this site come from a state extension page counted against the city's NOAA frost date, when we have transcribed one.

What is the frost date for cities in zone 6b?

There is no single frost date for zone 6b. Each city page uses the nearest NOAA station with a frost product. The table on this page lists the median last spring and first fall 32°F dates for the cities in this zone on this site. Those dates are 1991 to 2020 medians, not a forecast.

Zone source: USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map, ZIP tables from the PRISM Climate Group. A city's zone is the ZIP code whose centroid is nearest the city center. Frost dates: NOAA NCEI 1991 to 2020 climate normals.

Which Frost: Which Frost lists median last and first frost dates for U.S. cities from NOAA climate normals, with the USDA hardiness zone and a planting calendar only where a state extension page was transcribed. These are long term averages, not a forecast.
