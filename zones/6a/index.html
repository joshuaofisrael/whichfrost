USDA zone 6a: what to plant now

By Which Frost. Last verified 19 Nov 2026.

Zone 6a on the 2023 USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map is an average annual extreme minimum of -10 to -5°F. It is not a frost date and not a sowing date.

Frost dates inside zone 6a

USDA zone 6a is a winter minimum band. The frost dates below are NOAA 1991 to 2020 medians at 32°F for the 28 large cities on this site that fall in that band. They are medians, not guarantees for any single year.

Across 28 cities with a computed spring date, the earliest median last spring 32°F freeze is April 10 in Chicago, Illinois. The latest is May 7 in Aurora, Colorado.

NOAA's median growing season at 32°F in this zone runs from 151 days in Arvada, Colorado to 208 days in Chicago, Illinois.

What to plant now

Planting notes below are grouped by state, because extension calendars are not the same across a zone. With JavaScript on, the current month is marked inside any month list.

Colorado

Vegetable Planting Guide, CSU Extension GardenNotes #720 times these crops from the average last spring frost, not from the zone number. Counted dates are on each city page, using that city's NOAA 50 percent date. Source.

  • broccoli, cabbage, kohlrabi, onions, lettuce, peas, radish, spinach, turnips. As early as 2 to 4 weeks before the average last spring frost. Counted here as the span from 28 days before to 14 days before this city's NOAA 50 percent date.
  • beets, carrots, cauliflower, parsley, parsnips, potatoes, Swiss chard. As early as 0 to 2 weeks before the average last spring frost. Counted here as the span from 14 days before the NOAA 50 percent date through that date.
  • beans, celery, corn, cucumbers, New Zealand spinach, summer squash. From seed, around the average last spring frost. CSU also says cucumber and summer squash transplants without frost protection should wait until the frost potential is over. No day count is given for that wait, so none is calculated here.
  • lima beans, cantaloupe, eggplant, pepper, pumpkin, winter squash, tomato, watermelon. Typically 2 or more weeks after the average last spring frost. The date shown is 14 days after this city's NOAA 50 percent date, the start of that window, not a last planting date.

New Hampshire

Growing Vegetables: When to Plant Your Vegetable Garden, UNH Extension times these crops from the average last spring frost, not from the zone number. Counted dates are on each city page, using that city's NOAA 50 percent date. Source.

  • pea. Plant seed in the garden as soon as the soil thaws. UNH does not give a day offset, so no date is calculated.
  • broccoli, Brussels sprouts. Set out 2 to 4 weeks before the last frost. Start seed 4 to 6 weeks before transplanting.
  • cabbage, kale, kohlrabi, leek, onion. Safe to set out 4 weeks before the last frost.
  • lettuce. Safe to set out 3 to 4 weeks before the last frost. Start seed 4 to 5 weeks before transplanting.
  • spinach. Safe to set out 3 to 6 weeks before the last frost.
  • cauliflower. Safe to set out 2 weeks before the last frost.
  • potato. Plant seed potatoes 1 to 2 weeks before the last frost.
  • radish, turnip. Plant seed 2 to 4 weeks before the last frost.
  • Swiss chard. Plant seed 4 weeks before the last frost. Transplants are safe 2 weeks before the last frost.
  • bean. Plant seed in the garden after the last frost. The date shown is the NOAA 50 percent date, the first day of that window.
  • tomato. Safe to set out 1 to 2 weeks after the last frost. Start seed 6 to 8 weeks before transplanting.
  • pepper. Safe to set out 2 weeks after the last frost. Start seed 8 to 10 weeks before transplanting.
  • cucumber. Transplants are safe 1 to 2 weeks after the last frost. UNH also says to direct seed when the soil warms to 70°F. That soil temperature is not calculated here.
  • summer squash, zucchini. Safe to set out 2 weeks after the last frost.

Frequently asked questions

Does USDA zone 6a tell me what to plant this week?

No. Zone 6a is the 2023 USDA average annual extreme minimum temperature, -10 to -5°F. Nurseries use it for which perennial plants can take a typical winter. Vegetable sowing dates on this site come from a state extension page counted against the city's NOAA frost date, when we have transcribed one.

What is the frost date for cities in zone 6a?

There is no single frost date for zone 6a. Each city page uses the nearest NOAA station with a frost product. The table on this page lists the median last spring and first fall 32°F dates for the cities in this zone on this site. Those dates are 1991 to 2020 medians, not a forecast.

Zone source: USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map, ZIP tables from the PRISM Climate Group. A city's zone is the ZIP code whose centroid is nearest the city center. Frost dates: NOAA NCEI 1991 to 2020 climate normals.
