Sources and methods

By Which Frost. Last verified 16 Nov 2026.

Frost dates

The numbers are the NOAA NCEI U.S. Climate Normals for 1991 to 2020, annual and seasonal file, version 1.0.1 (archive dated 20230404). Retrieved 16 Nov 2026. Product page: U.S. Climate Normals. The probability definition is in the Normals Calculation Methodology 2020.

For spring, the published date is the chance the last freeze at that threshold happens on or after that date. For fall, it is the chance the first freeze happens on or before that date. We show 36°F, 32°F, and 28°F at 10, 50, and 90 percent. The growing season length is NOAA's own 50 percent figure for 32°F, the number of days the season lasts at least that long in half of years. We do not subtract the two dates to invent a length.

Special values stay special. -9999 with flag Y is "not computed." -6666 is "too rare to compute" (under a 10 percent chance the temperature occurs). -4444 is "year round risk."

Which station

The station inventory is the NCEI 30 year list. A city is matched to the nearest station that has a frost product, using the Census Bureau 2024 Gazetteer city point. The cap is 50 miles. Farther than that, the city is skipped. We do not blend two stations. The page shows how many years NOAA used and the completeness flag (S, R, P, or E) from the NCEI readme.

Which cities

Incorporated places in the Census Bureau Population Estimates Program file sub-est2024, population at least 100,000 in the 2024 estimate. Census place names that are consolidated governments are shown under the common city name, and the census name is printed on the page. Urban Honolulu is omitted: Honolulu International Airport does not have a computed 32°F date (flag Y), and the nearest station that does is about 110 miles away, on Maui.

Hardiness zones

The zone is the 2023 USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map value for the ZIP code whose centroid is nearest the city center, from the PRISM Climate Group ZIP tables and the Census 2024 ZCTA gazetteer. The match has to be within 30 miles or we leave the zone off. A neighborhood can sit in a different zone. We do not redraw the map.

Planting calendars

Colorado uses CSU Extension GardenNotes #720 (reviewed September 2024). Offsets are counted from the city's NOAA 50 percent last spring freeze. Soil temperature rules in that guide are not turned into dates, because we do not have those soil normals.

Georgia uses University of Georgia Extension Circular 943 (full review 10 March 2026), as the middle Georgia calendar. The north and south adjustments in that circular are quoted. They are not applied as a new set of dates.

New Hampshire uses the UNH Extension fact sheet "Growing Vegetables: When to Plant Your Vegetable Garden," retrieved 16 Nov 2026. Offsets are counted from the NOAA date, not from the Memorial Day example in that fact sheet.

Every other state has no transcribed calendar in this build. The city page says so.

Frequently asked questions

How does Which Frost pick a station?

A city is matched to the nearest NOAA station that has a frost product, using the Census Bureau 2024 Gazetteer city point. The cap is 50 miles. Farther than that, the city is skipped. Which Frost does not blend two stations.

Which planting calendars are transcribed?

Colorado uses CSU Extension GardenNotes #720. Georgia uses University of Georgia Extension Circular 943. New Hampshire uses the UNH Extension fact sheet on when to plant a vegetable garden. Every other state has no transcribed calendar in this build.

Which Frost: Which Frost lists median last and first frost dates for U.S. cities from NOAA climate normals, with the USDA hardiness zone and a planting calendar only where a state extension page was transcribed. These are long term averages, not a forecast.
