Alaska frost dates, and why zone 5a is not the frost date

By Which Frost. Last verified 20 Nov 2026.

Anchorage is the only Alaska city in this build. At Eagle Rvr Nature Ctr, AK US (USC00502642), 4.2 miles from the city center, NOAA's median last spring freeze at 32°F is May 16 and the median first fall freeze is September 20. The median growing season is 125 days. These are 1991 to 2020 medians, not a forecast and not a guarantee for a given year.

The station file lists 9 years for that 32°F element and completeness flag E. Flag E means NOAA estimated the normal from nearby stations that have a standard record. Elevation in the file is 158.5 m. Another station in the same area can sit at a different elevation or closer to water, and NOAA can publish a different date there. This site does not average stations. The Anchorage page has the 10 and 90 percent columns. How the station is chosen.

What the three Anchorage temperatures are called

At this station the median spring dates are May 31 at 36°F, May 16 at 32°F, and May 1 at 28°F. The NWS glossary describes frost as ice on surfaces, often while the thermometer is still in the middle 30s. A freeze is widespread air at 32°F or below, and a hard freeze is 28°F or lower for long enough that the glossary says it kills most vegetation. The hard freeze guide puts those three words next to the warning products. The Anchorage page has the full station table. Checked 20 Nov 2026.

UAF Cooperative Extension HGA-00134, checked the same day, says wind off the water keeps soil cool around Anchorage, the Kenai, and Bristol Bay, and that a frost pocket should be planted only with frost resistant vegetables. The seed start weeks from that publication are counted on the city page from the NOAA 50 percent date. They are not repeated here, and they are not a forecast.

Frequently asked questions

Why is Anchorage the only Alaska city on this site?

A city page is an incorporated place with a 2024 Census population of at least 100,000 whose nearest NOAA frost station is within 50 miles. Anchorage is the only Alaska place in this build, at a Census estimate of 289,600. This page does not add smaller Alaska towns by copying Anchorage's dates onto them.

Is the Anchorage frost date a full 30 year record at that station?

No. NOAA's file for Eagle Rvr Nature Ctr, AK US (USC00502642) lists 9 years on the 32°F spring element, with completeness flag E. Flag E means NOAA had at least 2 years and estimated the normal from nearby stations with a standard record. The dates are still the published 1991 to 2020 normal. They are medians, not a guarantee for this year.

Minneapolis is also zone 5a. Why is Anchorage's last frost later?

USDA zone 5a is the average coldest winter temperature, -20 to -15°F, not a spring frost date. On this site Minneapolis's median last 32°F freeze is April 23 and the season is 175 days. Anchorage's median is May 16 and the season is 125 days. The zone number does not move the frost date.
