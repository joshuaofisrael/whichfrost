Alabama frost dates run from February 21 in Mobile to March 24 in Huntsville

By Which Frost. Last verified 21 Nov 2026.

5 Alabama cities are on this site. The earliest median last spring freeze at 32°F is February 21 in Mobile. The latest is March 24 in Huntsville, 31 days later on the calendar. These are 1991 to 2020 medians at separate stations, not a forecast and not one date for the whole state.

A given year can freeze earlier or later than the median. This is not agricultural advice. NOAA's own growing season element, not a subtraction of these two dates, is in the table.

Five stations, not a state average

Each city keeps the nearest NOAA frost station within 50 miles. Which Frost does not blend them. Mobile (USW00013838, 3.7 miles from the city center) is the early row. Huntsville (USW00003856, 7.2 miles) is the late row. Birmingham sits between them: March 21, 28 days after Mobile. Montgomery is March 4 and Tuscaloosa is March 18. Station ids and distances are in the table so a reader can see the date belongs to that station, not to every backyard in the city.

What 33°F means next to these dates

Alabama Cooperative Extension (Shane Harris, posted 21 April 2021, checked 21 Nov 2026) says warm season vegetables, including tomatoes and peppers, can start to show leaf damage at 33°F. Some cool season crops, it says, can take 26 to 31°F for a limited time. After a fruit tree has flowered and set fruit, the same page says research it cites found that 10 percent of the fruit is killed at 28°F. Those are crop temperatures, not a sowing calendar, and this site does not invent one for Alabama.

The 32°F column in the table is the headline median. A 33°F leaf-damage line, as that extension page states it, sits closer to a frost in the middle 30s than to a hard freeze at 28°F. The NWS glossary describes frost as ice on surfaces, often while the air is still in the middle 30s. Birmingham's page is where the 36°F, 32°F, and 28°F medians for that station are printed side by side. How to protect plants from frost is the covers, soil water, and pots page, using this Alabama source plus Texas A&M and UC. The steps there do not replace a forecast, and they do not move the medians.

On 21 Nov 2026 the NCEI product page still called the 1991 to 2020 normals the latest set and said the 2023 update left agricultural normals unchanged. The dates above were not replaced. They are the 16 Nov 2026 station cache.

Frequently asked questions

Which Alabama city on this site has the latest median last frost?

Huntsville, at March 24 for the 50 percent last spring freeze at 32°F. Mobile is the earliest, at February 21. The calendar gap between those two medians is 31 days. Both figures are 1991 to 2020 medians at one station each. They are not a forecast, and they are not a guarantee for this year.

Birmingham and Huntsville are both zone 8a. Do they share a frost date?

No. USDA zone 8a is a winter minimum of 10 to 15°F, not a spring frost date. On this site Huntsville's median last 32°F freeze is March 24 and Birmingham's is March 21. Mobile is zone 9a and its median is February 21. The zone number does not move the frost date.

Does this page include an Alabama planting calendar?

No. Which Frost prints a vegetable calendar only where a state extension chart was transcribed, and Alabama does not have one in this build. The Alabama Cooperative Extension frost page cited here is about which temperatures hurt which crops, and about covers and watering. It is not turned into a sowing date. This is not agricultural advice.
